My story

“Fashion and sewing have always been around me. I first learned how to sew from my Dadi, and before I ever made clothes for myself, I was making them for my dolls. I didn’t do it to be creative at first, I did it because I had lost their store bought clothes and didn’t see the point in replacing them. I styled each doll based on who they were in my head. Pink, preppy skirts for Barbie. Capri jeans for my American Girl dolls. I spent hours watching videos about repurposing dolls, giving them new hair, makeup, and clothes, without realizing I was slowly teaching myself how to design.

I’m 14 years old and I’ve been sewing for five years. My aesthetic is heavily inspired by South Asian culture, especially Punjab, where the land itself is rural but the clothing is bright, colorful, and expressive, like seeing stars during the day. I’ve always been drawn to sparkles, warm oranges, pinks, and yellows, but I care just as much about elegance and detail. I started wearing jhumkis and bangles to school once I realized how much softness and craftsmanship they carried compared to the minimal jewelry I was used to seeing.

Growing up in SoCal shaped my love for baggy, streetwear silhouettes, while my appreciation for fabric, structure, and elegance comes from my mom and the women around me. I love mixing delicate materials with denim, leather, and deeper browns. Everything I make sits somewhere between formal and everyday, traditional and modern. My goal is to bring South Asian techniques, patterns, and embroidery into mainstream fashion in a way that feels wearable, natural, and personal.”

-Aashna Dhir
